Are Banks Open on MLK Day 2027?

Monday, 18 January 2027 is a US bank holiday. It is the Birthday of Martin Luther King, Jr. on the Federal Reserve Board's K.8 schedule, "Holidays Observed by the Federal Reserve System." The Reserve Banks are closed that day, so ACH, Fedwire, and check settlement wait until Tuesday, 19 January.

The holiday is the third Monday in January, not Dr. King's date of birth on 15 January. In 2027 that Monday is 18 January. The New York Stock Exchange lists the same close: "Monday, January 18." In 2026 the observance was Monday, 19 January, so a calendar copied forward from last year will be a day late.

Last and next settlement days

Friday, 15 January 2027 is still a Reserve Bank business day. If a payroll, rent payment, or wire must settle before the holiday, that Friday is the last day that counts. Saturday and Sunday are an ordinary weekend. Settlement starts again on Tuesday.

What still works on MLK Day

Cards, ATMs, and bank apps usually keep running, because those services are run by banks and card networks, not by the Reserve Banks' closing calendar. You can typically check a balance or take out cash if the funds are already in the account. That is not the same as moving money between banks. A mobile deposit may be accepted on Monday, but the clearing clock does not start until Tuesday. Branch lobbies are a third clock: most retail banks close counters on MLK Day, but there is no official national list of every lobby. Confirm an in-person errand with your own branch.

The rest of January 2027

January 2027 has only one other Federal Reserve holiday: Friday, 1 January, New Year's Day. The first settlement day of the year is Monday, 4 January. After that, the January weekdays that stay open are 4–8, 11–15, 19–22, and 25–29 January. Washington's Birthday, the next K.8 date, is Monday, 15 February.

Fed versus NYSE

Later in 2027 the Fed and the NYSE diverge, but they do not diverge on MLK Day. The NYSE is also closed on Monday, 18 January. By contrast, the NYSE is closed on Good Friday, 26 March, which is not a Federal Reserve holiday, and it observes Juneteenth on Friday, 18 June and Christmas on Friday, 24 December, while the Reserve Banks stay open those Fridays under the Saturday rule. For cash planning, treat 18 January as a full banking holiday.
